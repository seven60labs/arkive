Radio bands in one page

Radio frequency decides how a signal travels. Lower frequencies can bend around the earth or bounce off the upper atmosphere; higher ones travel in fairly straight lines.

BandRange of frequenciesGood forTypical reach
HF (high frequency)3 to 30 MHzBounces off the ionosphere, so it can reach hundreds or thousands of miles depending on time of day and the sun. Needs a long wire antenna. CB (27 MHz) and many ham bands live here.A few miles by ground wave; regional to worldwide by skywave
VHF (very high frequency)30 to 300 MHzMostly line of sight. Good outdoors, over water and in open country. MURS, marine VHF, NOAA weather and the ham 2 m band are here.Handheld to handheld: about 1 to 5 miles; much more from a hilltop or through a repeater
UHF (ultra high frequency)300 MHz to 3 GHzLine of sight, but short waves slip through buildings and around clutter better than VHF. FRS, GMRS and the ham 70 cm band are here.Handheld to handheld: about 0.5 to 2 miles in town, a few miles in the open; repeaters reach 20+ miles
Range is mostly about height, not watts. Getting the antenna higher (a hill, a roof, a mast) helps far more than doubling power. Box ratings like "36 miles" assume mountaintop to mountaintop.

Sources (checked November 2026): ARRL band plan; 47 CFR Part 95 (FCC Personal Radio Services).
