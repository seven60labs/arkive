When disaster hits: radio checklist

  1. Save power. Use the lowest power setting that works, keep the radio off between check-ins (follow the 3-3-3 plan), turn off the backlight and keep spare batteries warm and dry.
  2. Listen first. Tune NOAA weather and local broadcast AM/FM for official information. Then listen on your planned channel before talking.
  3. Get high. A window, a rooftop or a hill can double or triple your range.
  4. Keep messages short. Use this order, then let go of the button and listen:
    WHOwho you are (name or call sign) and who you are calling
    WHEREyour location: address, cross streets, landmarks or coordinates
    WHATwhat happened and how many people
    NEEDwhat you need: medical help, water, rescue, a message passed on
    Example: "This is Sam calling anyone on channel 3. I'm at 12 Oak Street by the school. Two adults, one with a broken leg. We need a medic. Over."
  5. Write it down. Note time, channel and what you heard.
  6. Know the emergency rule. If life or property is in immediate danger and nothing else works, FCC rules let you use any radio and any means to call for help (47 CFR 97.403 and 97.405 for amateur radio; 95.1731 and 95.531 for GMRS and FRS emergency messages). This is for real emergencies only, not for practice.

Sources (checked November 2026): 47 CFR Part 95 (FCC Personal Radio Services); 47 CFR Part 97 (FCC Amateur Radio Service); Ready.gov: emergency kit; FEMA.
