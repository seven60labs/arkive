FRS and GMRS

FRS (Family Radio Service) and GMRS (General Mobile Radio Service) share the same 22 channels in the 462 and 467 MHz UHF range. Most blister-pack "walkie-talkies" are FRS.

  • FRS needs no licence. Power is limited (2 W on most channels) and the antenna cannot be removed.
  • GMRS needs an FCC licence, but there is no exam. One licence covers you and your immediate family, lasts 10 years, and is applied for online through the FCC. GMRS radios may use more power, detachable antennas and repeaters.

Calling and emergency conventions

These are habits many groups follow, not FCC rules. Agree on a plan with your family and neighbours ahead of time.

  • Channel 1 is a common "first place to try" calling channel.
  • Channel 3 is widely suggested for emergency and preparedness check-ins, including the 3-3-3 listening plan.
  • Channel 20 (462.675 MHz) has a long history as an informal GMRS emergency and traveller-assistance channel; REACT volunteer teams have monitored it.
  • Privacy codes (CTCSS/DCS tones) do not make a channel private. They only hide other users from you. In an emergency, turn privacy codes off so you can hear everyone.
By FCC rule, any GMRS channel may be used for emergency messages, and emergency traffic always has priority (47 CFR 95.1731). FRS radios may also send one-way emergency messages (95.531).

Repeater basics (GMRS only)

A repeater is a radio on a hill or tower that listens on a 467 MHz input and re-transmits what it hears on the matching 462 MHz channel (for example, it listens on 467.5500 and repeats on 462.5500). That can stretch range from a couple of miles to 20 or more. To use one you need a GMRS licence, a radio with repeater ("duplex") support, the right tone, and usually the owner's permission. FRS-only radios cannot use repeaters.

Sources (checked November 2026): 47 CFR Part 95 (FCC Personal Radio Services); FCC: General Mobile Radio Service.
