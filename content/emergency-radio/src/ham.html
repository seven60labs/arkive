Amateur (ham) radio

Ham radio needs an FCC licence (the entry-level Technician exam covers VHF and UHF). It allows far more power and range than FRS or MURS, and trained volunteers (ARES, RACES) run emergency networks with it.

2 m and 70 cm basics

  • 2 m (144 to 148 MHz, VHF) and 70 cm (420 to 450 MHz, UHF) are where most handhelds work.
  • Simplex means radio to radio on one frequency. Range is like other handhelds: a few miles.
  • Repeaters on hills extend range to 20 to 50 miles. Local repeater frequencies, offsets and tones are listed by area; program them before you need them.
  • Identify with your call sign at least every 10 minutes and at the end of a contact.

HF emergency nets

On HF bands (for example 80 m and 40 m), licensed operators run scheduled nets: a net control station calls the roll and passes messages in order. After hurricanes and wildfires, regional emergency and health-and-welfare nets carry messages out of disaster areas. Times and frequencies change by region and season; your local ARES or radio club publishes them. Anyone can listen with a receiver.

In a real emergency where life or property is in immediate danger and no other way works, the FCC allows any means of radio communication to get help (47 CFR 97.403 and 97.405).
