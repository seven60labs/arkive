Breathing and meditation

These are simple attention and breathing exercises. They can help with stress, focus and sleep. Sit or lie comfortably. If you feel dizzy, stop and breathe normally.

Box breathing (2 to 4 minutes)

  1. Breathe in through the nose for a count of 4.
  2. Hold gently for 4.
  3. Breathe out slowly for 4.
  4. Hold for 4. Repeat.

If holding is uncomfortable, use 3 counts or skip the holds.

Slow calming breath

Breathe in for 4, out for 6, about 6 breaths a minute, for 3 to 5 minutes. A longer out-breath tends to calm the body. Useful before sleep or after a scare.

Body scan (5 to 10 minutes)

  1. Lie down, close your eyes, notice the breath.
  2. Bring attention to your toes; notice any sensation without changing it.
  3. Slowly move up: feet, ankles, legs, hips, belly, chest, hands, arms, shoulders, neck, face.
  4. If a part is tense, breathe out and let it soften.
  5. Finish with a few breaths feeling the whole body.

Simple mindfulness (any time)

  • One-minute pause: stop, take three slow breaths, notice what you can feel, hear and see.
  • 5-4-3-2-1: name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. Helpful for anxiety or panic.
  • Mindful walk: walk slowly, feeling each foot touch the ground.
  • When the mind wanders, that is normal: gently come back to the breath. That return is the practice.
If you have ongoing low mood, panic or trauma symptoms, these tools can help but are not a replacement for care. Talk to someone you trust or a health professional.
