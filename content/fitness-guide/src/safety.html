Start safely

Before you begin

Most people can safely start gentle exercise like walking and the beginner moves in this guide. Check with a health professional first if you have chest pain, heart disease, high blood pressure that is not controlled, diabetes, a lung condition, recent surgery or injury, dizziness or fainting, or if you are pregnant and new to exercise.

Warm up (5 minutes)

  1. March on the spot or walk briskly, 1 to 2 minutes.
  2. Arm circles, 10 forward and 10 back.
  3. Hip circles, 5 each way.
  4. 10 slow half squats.
  5. 10 gentle torso twists.
  6. Leg swings holding a wall, 10 each leg.

How hard to work: the talk test

EffortTalk testUse for
EasyYou can singWarm-up, recovery days
ModerateYou can talk, not singMost of your training
HardOnly a few wordsShort bursts, intermediate only

For strength moves, stop each set with 2 or 3 good reps left in the tank. Mild muscle burn and next-day soreness are normal.

Stop right away if you have

  • Chest pain, pressure or tightness
  • Unusual shortness of breath, dizziness, fainting or confusion
  • A racing or irregular heartbeat
  • Sharp, stabbing or joint pain (not ordinary muscle effort)
  • Numbness, tingling, or sudden weakness
Chest pain that does not go away with rest is an emergency. Get medical help.

Older adults

  • Balance work matters as much as strength: practise standing on one leg near a wall or chair.
  • Use a sturdy chair for support; do squats as sit-to-stands.
  • Move slowly between lying, sitting and standing to avoid dizziness.

Pregnancy

  • If you were active before, you can usually keep going at a comfortable level; if new, start with walking and gentle moves. Check with your midwife or doctor.
  • After about the first trimester, avoid long periods lying flat on your back; do bridges and floor work on your side or propped up.
  • Avoid contact sports, risk of falls, overheating and holding your breath.
  • Stop and seek care for bleeding, fluid leaking, regular painful contractions, calf pain or swelling, or dizziness.

Injuries and sore joints

  • Work around an injury, not through it: train the parts that do not hurt.
  • Pain up to about 3 out of 10 that settles by the next day is usually fine; more than that, ease off.
  • Swelling, locking, or giving way of a joint needs a professional look.

Children and teens

Kids need about an hour of active play a day. Bodyweight moves are fine for them with good form and no forcing; keep it playful.
